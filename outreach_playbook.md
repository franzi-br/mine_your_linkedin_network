# Outreach playbook

How to reach out, what to write, how often to follow up, and what to do after a conversation. Most of this comes from using the kit in a live senior search in October 2026. A few practices build on ideas from Laszlo Bock's *Apply Within* job search series; those are marked **(after Bock)** and described in our own words. See the [credits](README.md#credits).

## The principle

Ask for advice, not a job **(after Bock)**. Advice is cheap to give and flattering to be asked for. A job request puts the other person on the spot, and the easiest response is no response. Advice conversations still turn into roles: ours led to CV introductions, an interview and a webinar invitation within the first week.

## Before you reach out

1. **Have your materials ready** **(after Bock)**. Build the list whenever you like, but start sending only once your CV and your "why this company" story are done. When someone asks for your CV, send it the same day.
2. **Know your logistics.** Availability or end date, notice period, salary floor. Intermediaries asked for all three, and one would only act once she knew the end date.
3. **Check hard constraints first.** Location, office policy, level. Check what you can before the call and raise the rest early in it.

## Who to contact first

1. **Intermediaries and investors, for top seat roles.** VC talent partners and executive search directors gave the most leverage per conversation, because one person can carry your CV into several companies.
2. **One person per company.** Write to the stronger contact and hold the second until the first replies. Two messages landing at one firm the same week looks like a mass mailing.
3. **People outside the list.** Existing connections in other functions produced an interview lead and two of the first calls. Log them like any other contact.

## Writing the message

1. **Lead with something real about them.** A recent post, a shared employer, a mutual connection. A generic opener tells them the message could have gone to anyone.
2. **If someone referred you, say so in the first line** **(after Bock)**. It's the single best reason for a stranger to keep reading.
3. **Make the ask small and exact.** 15 minutes, or just names and pointers. When the ask was vague, one recipient replied asking whether we wanted paid coaching.
4. **Investors and board members: no call in the first message.** Ask for one tip and whether anyone in their founder network may be hiring soon. Suggest a call only if they offer one.
5. **Be honest about the relationship.** If they may not remember you, say it. If you owe an apology for a cancelled call or a dropped thread, start with it.
6. **Match the register.** Formal by default, informal where there is real shared history. In German, choose du or Sie per person and stick to it.
7. **Keep it under about 60 words.** Short messages get read on a phone between meetings.
8. **Check every name.** Dictated names came out wrong several times in our run. The prompt now checks names against the export.

## Follow up cadence

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Day 0 | Send in batches of 5 to 10 and log each send the same day. |
| Day 5 to 7, no reply | One short, friendly nudge. Then stop and mark "No reply". |
| After a "no" | A short thank you, no further ask. |
| Within 24 hours of a call | A thank you that mentions one concrete thing they said. |
| Same day as an intro | Tell the person who introduced you how it went. |
| When they commit to something | Put a "follow up by" date in the tracker and keep it. |
| A few weeks after the call | A short update on what you did with their advice **(after Bock)**. |

## In the conversation

1. **Respect the time you asked for.** Finish a little early rather than late.
2. **Bring two specific questions.** For example: what does your company look for in this hire; which teams are growing; who else should I talk to **(after Bock)**.
3. **Leave with a next step.** A name, a document to send, or a date to check back.

## Give something back

Look for what you know that is useful to them. In our run that meant passing contract leads to a recruiter between roles, sharing interim mandates with an interim manager, and co hosting a webinar on works councils for a VC's portfolio.

## Track every send

Log each message when you send it, not only the replies. Our first tracker logged only people who answered, which made the reply rate impossible to calculate. The kit's tracker has one row per message, a fixed status list and a source column, so you can see which kind of contact actually converts.
